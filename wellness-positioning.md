# Vibe Rise: Wellness Positioning & Regulatory Guide

## The Line That Matters

Vibe Rise is a **lifestyle movement**, not a health intervention. Everything we say, build, and pitch must stay on the left side of this line:

| Wellness / Lifestyle (our lane) | Health / Medical (not our lane) |
|---|---|
| "Feel more alive" | "Reduce anxiety" |
| "Build courage" | "Treat social anxiety" |
| "Daily practices for energy" | "Nervous system therapy" |
| "Track how you feel" | "Monitor your nervous system state" |
| "A movement for aliveness" | "A mental health intervention" |
| "Courage challenges" | "Exposure therapy" |
| "How are you feeling today?" | "What is your clinical state?" |
| "Gamified daily practice" | "Digital therapeutic" |
| "Community that values expression" | "Support group for mental health" |
| "Reference polyvagal theory" | "Prescribe from polyvagal theory" |
| "Joined a culture" | "Received treatment" |

The left column = Peloton, CrossFit, Wim Hof, Headspace, SoulCycle. Lifestyle brands. Unregulated.

The right column = medical device, health service, digital therapeutic. Regulated by FDA (US), MHRA (UK), TGA (Australia). Requires clinical evidence, licensed practitioners, and for minors, mandatory reporting and parental consent infrastructure.

**We are a gym for aliveness, not a clinic for anxiety.**

---

## Why This Matters

Crossing the line triggers:

1. **Medical device classification** — If an app claims to diagnose, treat, prevent, or cure a health condition, it may be classified as a medical device. This means FDA clearance (US), CE marking (EU), TGA registration (AU). Years of work. Clinical trials. Compliance teams.

2. **Health data regulation** — Health claims + data collection = health data. Health data is regulated under HIPAA (US), GDPR special categories (EU), and equivalent laws globally. The bar for consent, storage, access, and deletion is dramatically higher than for regular personal data.

3. **Duty of care** — If positioned as a mental health tool, and a user (especially a minor) discloses self-harm, suicidal ideation, or abuse, you may have mandatory reporting obligations. If you're a wellness app, this expectation doesn't attach in the same way.

4. **Advertising standards** — Health claims in marketing are regulated by the FTC (US), ASA (UK), ACCC (AU). "This app reduces anxiety" requires substantiation. "This app helps you feel more alive" does not.

5. **Insurance and liability** — Health positioning invites malpractice-adjacent claims. Wellness positioning doesn't.

## Polyvagal Theory: Our Position

Polyvagal theory (Stephen Porges, 1994) is a theoretical framework for understanding nervous system states. It is NOT a treatment protocol, NOT a regulated modality, and NOT a medical intervention.

**How we use it**: As a language framework for understanding felt states. The four states in our app (Vibe Rise / Safe / Activated / Shutdown) are self-reported felt experiences, not clinical diagnoses. They map loosely to polyvagal states (ventral vagal + sympathetic coherence / ventral vagal / sympathetic / dorsal vagal) but are presented as everyday language, not clinical terminology.

**What's safe**:
- Referencing polyvagal theory as the science behind why the practices work
- Using the framework to explain states in content, events, and the app
- Teaching people to notice their own states (self-awareness, not diagnosis)
- Thousands of yoga teachers, breathwork facilitators, coaches, and wellness brands use polyvagal language daily without regulatory issues

**What's not safe**:
- Claiming the app "assesses" or "diagnoses" a user's nervous system state
- Claiming polyvagal-based practices "treat" a specific mental health condition
- Positioning the 4-state check-in as clinical measurement rather than self-reflection
- Using polyvagal language to make health claims about outcomes for minors

**Note on scientific debate**: Some neuroscientists dispute aspects of polyvagal theory (particularly the phylogenetic claims). This doesn't affect our positioning because we use it as a felt-experience framework, not as a medical diagnostic model. We don't need it to be perfectly correct science. We need it to be a useful map for people understanding their own states.

## Language Guide

### Words and Phrases We Use

- Aliveness, alive, fully alive
- Courage, brave, bold
- Expression, play, freedom
- Practices, daily practices, habits
- Energy, capacity, bandwidth
- Felt states, how you feel, your experience
- Community, culture, movement, tribe
- Challenge, stretch, edge, comfort zone
- Maintenance, sustainability, daily rhythm
- Self-awareness, self-knowledge, self-discovery

### Words and Phrases We Avoid

- Therapy, therapeutic, treatment, intervention
- Diagnose, assess (clinically), measure (clinically)
- Anxiety disorder, depression, PTSD, trauma disorder
- Patient, client (in clinical sense)
- Prescription, protocol (in medical sense)
- Cure, heal (as a medical claim — "healing" as a personal journey is fine, "heals anxiety" is not)
- Clinical, clinical-grade, evidence-based (as product claims)
- Mental health tool, mental health app, mental health intervention

### Grey Zone Words (Use Carefully)

| Word | Safe usage | Unsafe usage |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| **Healing** | "A healing journey" / "Healing But Fun" (brand name, experiential) | "Heals trauma" / "Healing your PTSD" |
| **Nervous system** | "Your nervous system loves this" / "Nervous system practices" (casual, educational) | "Regulates your dysregulated nervous system" / "Nervous system therapy" |
| **Trauma** | "Many of us carry old patterns from difficult experiences" | "This app treats trauma" / "Trauma therapy" |
| **Regulation** | "Self-regulation practices" / "Find your rhythm" | "Regulates your autonomic nervous system" (clinical framing) |
| **Safe / Safety** | "Feeling safe in your body" / "Safety as a felt experience" | "Clinically safe" / "Safety-tested intervention" |
| **Rewire** | "Rewire old patterns" (metaphorical, aspirational) | "Rewires your neural pathways" (medical claim) |

## Minors: What We Need to Do

### Events (Low Risk)

Silent disco events, pop-ups, workshops where teenagers attend are **standard entertainment/community events**. No different from a SoulCycle class, a Wim Hof workshop, or a CrossFit teen program. No special regulatory requirements beyond normal event safety.

- Parental consent for under-16s attending events = good practice, not always legally required depending on jurisdiction
- No health data collected at events
- QR code at the end leads to app signup, where age-gating happens

### App (Medium Risk — Needs Work)

If teenagers use FindMyFlow, data protection laws apply:

**Age-gating at signup (required)**:
- Ask date of birth at registration
- Under 13: Block signup entirely (COPPA in US is strict, fines up to $50K per violation)
- 13-15: Require parental consent before account creation (GDPR Article 8, varies by EU country)
- 16-17: Can create account, but with enhanced privacy defaults (UK Age Appropriate Design Code)
- 18+: Standard flow

**Data minimization for minors**:
- Don't collect more data than necessary from under-18 users
- The daily 4-state check-in is self-reported mood tracking, not health data, AS LONG AS we don't frame it as clinical
- Quest completions, Wahoo challenges, streak data = behavioral/engagement data, not health data
- Zarlo conversations from minors: be careful. If AI coaching asks about feelings and a minor discloses something serious, what's the protocol? Define this before launching a teens-facing version.

**Privacy defaults for minors**:
- No profiling for advertising
- No data sharing with third parties
- High privacy settings by default
- Easy deletion of account and all data

**What we DON'T need (if we stay in the wellness lane)**:
- HIPAA compliance (US) — only applies if we're a covered entity making health claims
- Clinical ethics board approval
- Licensed practitioner oversight
- Mandatory reporting infrastructure (unless we actively position as mental health)

### Content and Marketing (Medium Risk)

When talking about Vibe Rise and teenagers in marketing, pitches, or content:

**Do say**:
- "A culture your kids can grow up inside"
- "Events where they feel alive"
- "A community that values courage over performance"
- "Daily practices for energy and confidence"
- "The thing they're scared of is usually the thing worth doing"
- "Duolingo for feeling alive"
- "A gym for aliveness"

**Don't say**:
- "Helps your teenager's mental health"
- "Treats adolescent anxiety"
- "Nervous system intervention for young people"
- "Therapeutic tool for teens"
- "Addresses childhood trauma"
- "Designed for at-risk youth"

**The pitch to a dad**: "This is a movement your sons can grow up inside" — NOT "this app will help your sons' mental health." Same outcome. Completely different regulatory exposure.

## The Pitch Positioning (For Business Partners, Investors, Sponsors)

### How We Describe Ourselves

**One-liner**: "Vibe Rise is a lifestyle movement for aliveness. Events, an app, and a creator community that helps people spend more of their lives in the state where life is actually worth living."

**Category**: Wellness / Lifestyle / Community — NOT health tech, mental health, digital therapeutics

**Comparables**: Peloton (fitness community + content + app), CrossFit (methodology + community + box model), Wim Hof Method (science-backed practice + events + app), Headspace (daily practice app + content) — NOT BetterHelp, Talkspace, Calm (when it positions as clinical)

**Revenue model language**: "Subscription app + equipment (headsets) + creator tools + events" — NOT "digital therapeutic + patient acquisition + outcomes tracking"

### When the Science Comes Up

Investors and partners will ask about the science. This is good — the science is a differentiator. But frame it correctly:

**Say**: "The practices are informed by polyvagal theory, flow research, and memory reconsolidation science. We use these frameworks to design experiences that help people access states of safe activation. The science gives us a design language, not a prescription."

**Don't say**: "We've built a clinically-validated nervous system regulation tool based on polyvagal therapy."

**If asked "Is this evidence-based?"**: "The underlying science (polyvagal theory, Csikszentmihalyi's flow research, Ecker's memory reconsolidation) is well-established. We apply these frameworks to experience design and daily practice. We're a practice platform, not a clinical tool, so our evidence is user outcomes and community growth, not clinical trials."

## Checklist: Staying in the Wellness Lane

### Before Launching Any Feature

- [ ] Does the feature description avoid health/medical claims?
- [ ] Is user data from this feature categorized as behavioral/engagement, not health data?
- [ ] Could a reasonable person interpret this feature as medical/clinical? If yes, reframe.
- [ ] If minors could use this feature, is age-gating in place?
- [ ] Does the AI (Zarlo) have guardrails for sensitive disclosures? (e.g., "I want to hurt myself" should route to a crisis helpline, not generate a coaching response)

### Before Publishing Any Content

- [ ] Does the content avoid claiming to treat, cure, diagnose, or prevent a medical condition?
- [ ] Is polyvagal theory referenced as a framework, not prescribed as a treatment?
- [ ] If the content mentions teenagers/minors, does it frame Vibe Rise as culture/community/events, not therapy?
- [ ] Would this content survive an FTC/ASA review for health claims?

### Before Any Pitch or Partnership Conversation

- [ ] Are we describing ourselves as wellness/lifestyle, not health tech?
- [ ] Are comparables lifestyle brands (Peloton, CrossFit, Wim Hof), not health platforms (BetterHelp, Talkspace)?
- [ ] Is the "kids" angle framed as culture and experiences, not mental health intervention?
- [ ] If asked about evidence, are we pointing to frameworks and community outcomes, not clinical claims?

## If We Ever Want to Cross the Line (Future Consideration)

There may come a point where clinical positioning makes strategic sense (e.g., insurance reimbursement, institutional partnerships, school mental health programs). If so:

- This is a multi-year, multi-million dollar regulatory path
- Requires clinical trials, IRB approval, FDA/MHRA/TGA submission
- Requires a clinical advisory board and licensed practitioners
- Requires HIPAA/health data compliance infrastructure
- Requires a completely separate product or product tier with clinical-grade consent, data handling, and outcomes tracking
- Could be built as a "Vibe Rise Clinical" tier alongside the wellness product, not a replacement

**For now**: Stay firmly in the wellness lane. The market is massive, the regulatory burden is minimal, and the mission is served just as well. We don't need to be a health product to change lives.
